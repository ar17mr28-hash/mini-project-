AquaSentinel: Project Defense Guide

Structured academic defense resource covering architecture, computer vision mathematics, implementation boundaries, and oral defense Q&A.

01. Problem Statement

Marine debris, abandoned fishing gear ("ghost nets"), submerged industrial pipelines, and navigational hazards threaten benthic marine ecosystems and vessel safety. While autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and tow-fish equipped with side-scan sonars collect extensive underwater acoustic imagery, manual inspection by marine technicians is:

  • Labor-Intensive: Hours of monotonous inspection of raw acoustic swaths.
  • Fatigue-Prone: Human operators easily miss subtle debris against textured seabed ripple patterns.
  • Real-time Latency: Lack of automated edge triage delays mission response time.

Challenge: Underwater optical cameras fail beyond a few meters due to turbidity, scattering, and light attenuation. Sonar is effective, but acoustic speckle noise and dynamic range falloff make automated interpretation difficult without specialized computer vision pipelines.

02. Proposed Solution

AquaSentinel provides an automated acoustic image analysis workstation that takes raw or preprocessed side-scan sonar frames, normalizes local acoustic backscatter variations, suppresses speckle noise, extracts high-frequency boundaries, segments anomalous contrast clusters, and frames candidate regions in bounding boxes with heuristic scoring.

05. Image Processing Methodology (The 8 OpenCV Steps)
  1. Image Ingestion: Safe decoding of uploaded raster formats (PNG, JPG) into multi-dimensional NumPy arrays ($H \times W \times C$).
  2. Grayscale Conversion: Reduction of redundant 3-channel colour data into a single luminance channel $I(x,y)$, aligning with physical acoustic backscatter.
  3. Gaussian Blur ($\mathbf{5\times 5}$ Kernel): Convolving with 2D Gaussian kernel to attenuate multiplicative acoustic speckle noise caused by underwater sound interference.
  4. CLAHE Equalization: Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization operates on local $8 \times 8$ pixel tiles with a contrast clip limit of $2.0$ to prevent noise amplification in uniform sediment zones.
  5. Canny Edge Detection: Dual-threshold hysteresis tracking based on median pixel luminance ($0.66\tilde{I} \to 1.33\tilde{I}$) extracting structural gradients.
  6. Dual-Band Thresholding: Isolating both specular acoustic highlights ($I \ge \mu + 1.8\sigma$) and acoustic shadow voids ($I \le \mu - 1.8\sigma$).
  7. Morphological Filtering & Contours: $7 \times 7$ morphological opening and closing to bridge fragmented acoustic shadows followed by border-following contour detection.
  8. Noise Filtering & Bounding Box Annotation: Filtering candidate contours under $300\text{ px}$ to suppress residual speckles, extracting bounding rectangles $[x, y, w, h]$, and computing the heuristic anomaly score.

07. Known Limitations & Engineering Honesty

To maintain scientific integrity during project defense, acknowledge the following physical constraints:

  • Heuristic Sensitivity: Statistical thresholding operates best on relatively uniform acoustic backscatter. Complex rocky seabed outcrops may generate false positive candidate highlights.
  • No Object Semantic Understanding: Traditional CV identifies "salient high-contrast geometric anomalies", but cannot semantically distinguish a tire from a circular rock without a deep-learning classifier.
  • 2D Projection: The system analyzes 2D side-scan waterfall imagery; 3D object height must be inferred trigonometrically from shadow length and vehicle altitude.
08. Faculty Oral Defense Q&A Cheatsheet
Examiner Prep
Q1: Why did you use CLAHE instead of standard Global Histogram Equalization?

Answer: Side-scan sonar images suffer from non-linear acoustic falloff—the nadir (directly beneath the fish) is saturated while the outer swath edges have lower intensity. Global equalization over-amplifies noise in the dark edges and blows out highlights. CLAHE computes equalization in small localized tiles with a contrast limit, preserving fine acoustic shadow details uniformly.

Q2: How do you justify calling this an anomaly detection system if you didn't train a CNN?

Answer: In computer vision, anomaly detection has a rich mathematical foundation in statistical salience and statistical deviation from background models. Our system identifies spatial, contrast, and gradient outliers relative to the seabed baseline. We explicitly describe them as "candidate anomaly regions" and compute a heuristic score, avoiding misleading claims of AI classification while delivering genuine automation.

Q3: How does the system handle small noise speckles?

Answer: Noise is suppressed at three distinct stages: (1) $5\times 5$ Gaussian blur prior to gradient extraction, (2) morphological opening ($7\times 7$ kernel) that eliminates isolated foreground dots, and (3) geometric area filtering in contour analysis which ignores components with area under $300\text{ px}$.
